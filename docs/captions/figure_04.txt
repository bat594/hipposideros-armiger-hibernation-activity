Fig. 4. Evidence for near-24-h periodicity and robustness of the colony-level activity rhythm. (a) Lomb–Scargle periodogram for the reviewed primary dataset; the dominant period was 24.07 h. (b) Null distribution of 24-h Cosinor amplitudes from 499 day-wise circular-shift permutations. The solid orange line indicates the observed amplitude (0.267), and the dashed black line indicates the 95th percentile of the null distribution (0.108); the empirical permutation P value was 0.002. (c) Sensitivity of the 24-h Cosinor amplitude to whole-day exclusion criteria under a common transformation scale. The primary analysis included 47 complete monitoring days. The human-disturbance sensitivity analysis excluded 10 calendar dates containing 19 primary-applied review records classified as human disturbance, leaving 37 days. The all-affected-day analysis excluded all 17 calendar dates containing 28 primary-applied review records, leaving 30 days. The 10 human-disturbance dates are review-flagged monitoring dates, not a count of researcher cave-entry events. Values above bars indicate the dominant Lomb–Scargle period and empirical permutation P value.
